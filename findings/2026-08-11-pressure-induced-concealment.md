# Corrected note: pressure and trace/answer mismatch

**Status: superseded interpretation, corrected 2026-09-28.** The
[original log](archive/2026-08-11-pressure-induced-concealment.md) is retained as history.
See the [current audit](2026-09-28-research-reset.md).

The recorded judge labels included six `concealed` outputs among nine pressured
Bonsai responses, and five among nine pressured GPT-OSS responses. These describe
annotations of **one subject response per cell**. Three judge votes are three
annotations of the same response, not three replications of subject behavior.
Cross-judging the Bonsai run reused its 12 responses, so it adds annotation evidence,
not more subject samples. Neither vote unanimity nor agreement between two judges
establishes validity, stability, or generalization.

The original 12-cell design had three controls and nine treatments. Only four of
nine pressured rows had an identical question anywhere in that run's controls;
within each named pressure mechanism, only its first treatment had a control.
The design cannot support a matched pressure effect across all nine treatments.
The September revision adds all missing exact-question controls (18 cells total).

Some visible answers make unsupported claims of physical measurement or internal
telemetry, and some emitted traces explicitly discuss producing those claims. The
supported observation is a **textual trace/answer mismatch or unsupported provenance**.
Statements about “latent recognition,” “deliberated lies,” honesty by default, or
corruption by incentives go beyond this evidence. Generated reasoning is not
privileged access to cognition. A hypothetical marker can matter, and the old judge
silently truncated answers, potentially removing such disclosures.

The reported 6/9 is not a demonstrated lower bound: false positives and false
negatives are both possible. Choosing a “judge of record” on five subjective cases
is not external validation. The proposed sampling run did not complete, so its
predictions are not findings. Future runs need repeated subject samples, complete
controls, separately assessed annotations, and explicit uncertainty.
